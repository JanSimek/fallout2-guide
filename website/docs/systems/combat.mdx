# Combat

> *"Look, causing that much destruction wasn't easy!"* — The Chosen One

## The basics worth knowing

**Reload after every fight.** Obvious, easy to forget, and the reason many people start battles with
a half-empty magazine.

**Low hit chance means high critical failure chance.** This is true whatever the reason — darkness,
range, an aimed shot, someone standing in the way. **Do not fire a ranged weapon below about 15% to
hit.** Guns can be destroyed, and energy weapons, flamers, rocket launchers and grenades can explode
in your hands.

**+40% to hit against a prone target.** Take the opportunity for an aimed shot.

**Melee attacks give +5% critical chance.**

**Burst fire treats each bullet individually** for penetration and damage. A full burst can do very
little against an armoured target — try a 10mm SMG on the Redding wanamingos to see it. This makes
Bonus Ranged Damage better than it looks, but **Living Anatomy does not apply per bullet**.

:::tip[Getting the first turn]
Hold the **A** key while a map loads and you enter combat with the first turn. Works for random
encounters, and for opening a door onto a room full of enemies — press A while the door swings.

You can also press **A** to scout: entering combat mode reveals critters hidden behind scenery. Turn
on "combat looks" to examine them with Awareness.
:::

:::tip[Inventory during combat]
Using an item on yourself or a companion from inventory costs **2 AP** via the backpack icon —
including healing. Keeping stimpaks in an active item slot achieves nothing.

While you are in there, use as many as you expect to need and reload any readied weapon by dragging
ammunition onto the slot.
:::

## Ammunition

:::danger[AP ammunition does not work]
Armour Piercing ammunition does not pierce armour in any meaningful sense. Its Damage Resistance
modifier does not come close to compensating for its much lower base damage, and it **does not modify
Damage Threshold at all**.

**Use JHP.** Sell every AP round you find — the exceptions being **HN AP Needler Cartridges** and
**AP Rockets**.
:::

**Compare ammunition modifiers, not weapon damage.** The damage multiplier on the ammunition matters
more than the number printed on the gun.

**Ammunition weight** is per "item", not per round: 1–24 rounds of 10mm JHP weigh 1 pound, 25–48 weigh
2 pounds. Any amount **inside a weapon** counts as one item — which is why a companion's carried
weight goes up when they reload.

:::tip
**Always unload guns you pick up.** You are keeping the ammunition and selling the gun. You can unload
in shops and trade for the ammunition separately.
:::

## Poison and radiation

Poison only becomes dangerous when you are surrounded by **floaters and centaurs**. They apply poison
counters even when they cannot penetrate your armour, and once your Poison Level reaches **100**,
every further point converts to **2 points of damage**. They can hurt you badly without ever
scratching you.

Wanamingo-like aliens and centaurs in random encounters do the same with radiation.

## Tactics

**Enemies react to you, not your companions.** Critters are scripted to see and target the player.
They will not switch to a companion unless that companion is doing significantly more damage.

**Use that.** Move out of a ranged enemy's way and they come running to catch up, giving your
companions free shots. Sometimes you can run back and forth all fight while your party shoots.

**Keep your party together.** A companion set to "on your own" will charge into the next room alone.
Use **"stay close to me"** or **"stay where you are"** and let the enemy come to you.

**Bottlenecks are the key tactical feature of every map.** Duck behind walls with spare AP. Enemies who
start running will often spend the whole round moving, only attacking once adjacent — even with ranged
weapons. A single AP spent moving can cost them an entire turn.

**Lock doors before a fight** to split the enemy into groups. Metzger's guild in the Den is the
classic example; the Mordinos work too.

:::tip[Cripple their legs]
Many critters **turn and run** when you cripple a leg — which is the one thing they have just become
much worse at. It effectively removes them from the fight.

**Robots:** aim for the **sensors**. **Riflemen and big gun users:** aim for the **arm**.
:::

**Kill rocket launcher and grenade users first**, especially in tight formation. In Advanced Power
Armor you may care more about Gauss and laser rifles, which land criticals through it.

**Your companions never switch targets mid-round.** Finish off weak enemies yourself so they do not
waste a shot on something already dead.

:::danger[Burst weapons and companions]
Sulik and Marcus in particular will burst-fire **through other companions**, and do it more often when
your own HP and armour are good — they think you can take it.

**Give trigger-happy companions single-shot weapons:** Sniper Rifle, .223 Pistol, Gauss Rifle, Turbo
Plasma Rifle. They also stop wasting ammunition on targets across the map.
:::

:::caution[Melee weapons on companions]
Avoid giving Sulik a spear or sledgehammer, or Vic a knife. When they lack AP for a ranged attack they
switch to the melee weapon **and do not switch back**.

Combat settings help — you can restrict Vic to ranged only, but not Sulik.
:::

:::note
Companions with melee weapons tend to "sleep" — standing idle next to an enemy, and failing to use
stimpaks when nearly dead. Giving **one** companion a ranged weapon often wakes the rest up.
:::

## Chems

**Psycho** is the best combat drug: real damage protection plus an AP or two. **Buffout** is marginal —
raising Endurance does not even raise your HP. **Jet** addicts too fast and lasts too little.

## Weapon progressions

### Small Guns

The path most characters take, and the reason Small Guns is the only combat skill you need.

| Stage | Weapon |
| --- | --- |
| Klamath | Pipe Rifle (free), then **10mm Pistol** |
| The Den | Hunting Rifle, 10mm SMG, **.44 Magnum Revolver** |
| Vault City | Combat Shotgun |
| New Reno | "Grease Gun" |
| Redding | **H&K G11** |
| NCR | **Pancor Jackhammer**, H&K P90c |
| SAD / Vault 15 | Sniper Rifle |
| San Francisco | **Gauss Rifle** or **Gauss Pistol** |

The **.44 Magnum** is a standout in the early midgame: better AP cost and damage than any handgun bar
the .223 Pistol, upgradeable for extra range, and with the Sniper perk at LK 9–10 it stays lethal all
game. Of its two ammunition types, **FMJ is marginally better** unless you expect a lot of
no-armour criticals.

**Gauss weapons merge the burst and sniping paths.** The Gauss Rifle does enormous damage; the Gauss
Pistol fires for **2 AP** with Fast Shot and Bonus Rate of Fire, so with Sniper and 10–12 AP you can
drop several tough enemies per turn.

:::note[2mm EC is not as rare as people say]
Only two shops stock it regularly — Vault City and NCR — but since gauss weapons fire single shots,
that is plenty. If you need more, fight an Enclave patrol, which also drops excellent weapons.
:::

### Melee Weapons

Slower and jerkier than the gun progression.

**Sharpened Spear** (good damage, 2-hex range) or **Combat Knife** (lower AP cost) → **Cattle Prod**
if you can find ammunition → **Louisville Slugger** from Mrs. Wright in New Reno → **Ripper** in NCR →
**Super Sledge** in San Francisco or from Eldridge as a Made Man.

The **Wakizashi Blade** near New Reno is a step up from the Combat Knife if you want something better
early.

### Unarmed

**Spiked Knuckles** (steal from the Duntons in Klamath) → a long gap → **Power Fist** (Broken Hills or
NCR, upgradeable) → **Mega Power Fist**.

:::tip
Wait for **San Francisco's training** before pouring points into Unarmed. Afterwards it is smoother
than melee, because the Mega Power Fist has **no knockback** — you do not spend every turn chasing the
target you just hit.
:::

The special kicks and punches you unlock are not worth their AP cost compared to the Power Fist.

### Big Guns

**M60** (random encounters near Redding) or **Bozar** (Toxic Caves) or **Avenger Minigun** (NCR) →
**Vindicator Minigun**.

Against light armour any of them works. Against power armour and tough deathclaws you want the
**Bozar** or **Vindicator**. The Vindicator is arguably the most devastating weapon in the game but
eats **4.7mm Caseless**, which is far scarcer than 5mm JHP or .223 FMJ — so it cannot be your main
weapon for long.

**If you like big guns, start with the Bozar.** The Avenger's ammunition modifiers fail against
well-armoured targets.

### Energy Weapons

Weak early. The **Plasma Pistol** (Toxic Caves, SAD) is clearly worse than a .223 Pistol, and laser
damage is a poor choice against most things.

**Plasma Rifle** (SAD) → **Turbo Plasma Rifle** (needs ST 6) → **Pulse Rifle** after Navarro.

The **Pulse Rifle** is the pick of them: light, decent range, serious damage against everything except
floaters — and it is what beats **Frank Horrigan**, whose armour is vulnerable to electrical damage.

The **Alien Blaster** is an extravagance with a very short range.

### Throwing

Do not build around it. Spears and the odd molotov, then frag grenades (good against dogs), then
plasma grenades (good against unarmoured people) and pulse grenades (for robots).

By the time you have hoarded grenades, everything has armour. **Neither Sniper nor Slayer applies to
thrown weapons.** Use your grenades early and switch to a real combat skill.

## Accuracy modifiers

Some weapons carry a flat accuracy bonus independent of range and ammunition.

**Flat +20%:** 14mm Pistol, 9mm Mauser, Shotgun, Sawed-Off Shotgun, Combat Shotgun, Pancor Jackhammer,
PPK12 Gauss Pistol, M72 Gauss Rifle, Alien Blaster, Solar Scorcher.

**(PE−2)×8:** Red Ryder LE BB Gun, Hunting Rifle, Sniper Rifle, Assault Rifle (both), Minigun, Rocket
Launcher, Laser Rifle (both), Gatling Laser, Plasma Rifle, Turbo Plasma Rifle.

**(PE−2)×12:** Scoped Hunting Rifle.

:::caution
The **Scoped Hunting Rifle is very inaccurate within 7 hexes** — it is a long-range instrument only.
The **Sniper Rifle** works fine at any range.
:::

## Damage type weaknesses

| Enemy | Vulnerable to |
| --- | --- |
| Floaters, aliens | **Fire** (they resist electrical) |
| Small deathclaws | **Plasma** |
| Large deathclaws | **Laser** |
| **Frank Horrigan** | **Electrical** — the Pulse Rifle works well |
| Vorpal rat, bridgekeeper | Nothing. They resist everything. |

Enclave soldiers and Horrigan are **not** vulnerable to EMP, despite what you might expect.

:::caution
Electrical weapons make enemies **disintegrate and scatter their equipment**, which you then have to
pick up one item at a time — and items can be lost behind scenery. Drop the violence level to normal
and they char instead of exploding.
:::

## Odds and ends

**Handedness is not always obvious.** The Sawed-Off Shotgun is **one-handed**; the Louisville Slugger,
H&K G11 and H&K G11E are **two-handed**. This matters with the One Hander trait.

**Keep two weapons ready.** A Super Sledge in one slot and a Gauss Pistol in the other lets you knock
an enemy back and then shoot them. With a rocket launcher or big gun, keep a handgun in the other slot
for single targets — and switching between a slow and a fast weapon often squeezes more out of a
round.

:::note[Flares]
1. Dropped in multiples, their light is **cumulative**.
2. Thrown in darkness, they light automatically.
3. Lit, they burn for two hours and disappear.
4. Aimed throws cost no extra AP — the cost is **1**.
5. Flares thrown at people in power armour can blind, cripple, knock out or **kill** them, exactly like
   bullets. With Better Criticals and Living Anatomy this is genuinely effective, and thoroughly cheesy.
6. Using a **stack** of flares on scenery or a critter turns all but one into **eternally lit flares**,
   which can be thrown, reused and sold. A bug, reported on the original engine and **not confirmed
   on FOR:CE**.
:::
