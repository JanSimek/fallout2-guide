# Random Encounters

:::caution[RPU raises the encounter rate significantly]
This is deliberate. killap's intention was to restore the game's original feel, in which venturing
into the wastes was genuinely dangerous. Early on it is a real nuisance.

**Read the scout manuals and put a few points into Outdoorsman** and it stops being a problem. The
rate can also be adjusted in the configuration if you find it unbearable.
<Vanilla>The base encounter rate is much lower.</Vanilla>
:::

RPU also **adds** encounters. Many are opportunities rather than threats — a caravan fending off
robbers, for instance, lets you wait for the robbers to run dry and then collect weapons you could not
buy for hours yet.

## Avoiding them

Every encounter rolls **Outdoorsman** to see whether you can decline it.

:::note
Your **effective Outdoorsman caps at 95%** before penalties. There is no point raising it further, or
carrying a Motion Sensor once you are there.

XP from a successful avoidance is `100 + encounter difficulty (0–70, by zone) − your skill (max 95)`.
In the car you often get no XP at all, which suggests the car gives an independent **~50%** chance of
avoidance on top.
:::

**Ending an encounter:** run to the edge of the map, or use the car — which works **even mid-combat**.
Running off the map puts you in the car if you have one.

:::danger[Do not drop anything on an encounter map]
Items left behind when you exit to the world map are **gone forever**. **This includes companions.**
:::

## What lives where

The zones overlap heavily, and mantises, wolves and radscorpions turn up almost everywhere.

| Region | Encounters |
| --- | --- |
| **Northwest** (Arroyo) | Scorpions, radscorpions, outcasts, fishermen (coast), war party, hunting party, cannibals, wilders, hermit, rats, pig rats, nomads, silver geckos, holy people, ants, spore plants |
| **North** (Klamath, Den, Modoc) | Highwaymen, golden geckos, mole rats, wild brahmin, slavers, trappers, Den caravan, Vault City caravan, merchants and guards, farmers, homeless people, bandits, moonshiners, mantis, rave party, robbers |
| **Northeast** (Vault City, Gecko) | Merchant party, wild dogs, ghoul crazies, patrol unit, mutated rats, mutated mole rats, mutated pig rats, ghoul scavengers, caravan |
| **East** (Broken Hills, NCR) | Marauders, raiders, Rangers, caravan, Unity patrol, rogues |
| **Central** (Redding, New Reno) | Yakuza, mobsters, gangs, traveler, fire geckos, claim jumpers, homesteaders, prospectors, bootleggers, Redding caravan, wolves, New Reno caravan |
| **South** (Military Base, Vault 13) | Remnants of the Master's army, deathclaws, NCR Rangers, caravan |
| **Southwest** (Navarro, San Francisco) | Floaters, centaurs, Enclave patrols, Hubologists, press gang, aliens, caravan, mercenaries |

:::note[RPU additions]
Nomad encounters now include a **shaman and chieftain**. Homesteader encounters include **children**,
and you can now **save homesteaders** and be rewarded for it. Hubologists in random encounters turn
**hostile** if you wiped out the Hubologists in San Francisco.
<Vanilla>None of these variants exist.</Vanilla>
:::

## Combined encounters

**"X fending off Y"** — stay out of the way and neither side attacks you until they are done.

:::caution
Enter a cave and come back out during one of these and **they forget they were fighting**. Normally
hostile groups then turn on you.
:::

**"Aliens and deathclaws"** and similar: you fight one group while the other stays idle until combat
ends. Use the gap to leave, or to position yourself.

## Merchants

Whether a merchant will trade depends on the **sign of your karma** — some refuse do-gooders, others
refuse villains. Their stock reflects their region: New Reno merchants carry drugs, Vault City
merchants carry healing items.

:::tip[Worth hunting]
- A caravan in the **NCR–Vault 13** area is a reliable source of **Scout Handbooks**.
- A caravan in the **San Francisco** area has a **10% chance** of carrying the rare **XL70E3** each
  time you meet it.
- Some merchants around **Broken Hills** carry **uranium ore**, so you can do the refinery quest
  without finding Chuck Stodgers.
:::

:::note
**The hunting party, Hubologists, bootleggers and moonshiners** all let you steal freely.
:::

:::danger
In the **homeless people** and **farmers** encounters there is sometimes a bald male critter in
"yellow pyjamas" who counts as a **child** — you can tell from his floating dialogue. Killing him makes
you a **Childkiller**.
:::

## The rave party

Found around the Den, and only between **19:00 and midnight**.

:::tip[Infinite chems]
One or two partygoers sell booze; others sell Mentats, Buffout and Psycho. When you pay, your money
goes into their inventory and they walk over to use a **newly created dose** on you. **Move away** and
the dose is left on the ground for you to pick up instead.

Repeat as long as you like, then kill them or steal the money back.
:::

## Bounty hunters

Drop below **−501 karma** (posters appear at −500) or become a **Childkiller** and bounty hunters start
hunting you. Groups of **two to four**, plus one if you killed children, plus one if you are level 19+.
Their equipment scales with your level.

Each kill costs you **−10 karma**, which is a downward spiral. If you are only wanted for karma, climb
back above −501 and the posters disappear.

## Robber caves

Many mountain encounters have a cave opening. Most contain radscorpions, mole rats, geckos or
deathclaws, none worth fighting — but there is a **1 in 13** chance per cave, plus a Luck check, of
finding a cave full of **human robbers**.

They carry a great deal of ammunition, and each cave has **three chests** of money, stimpaks and
grenades. With luck: a **Super Sledge** from level 6, a **Power Fist** from level 8, a **Plasma Rifle**
from level 11. Well worth taking on with companions.

:::note
An empty cave means you failed the Luck check.
:::

## Levelling up

The best place late in the game is **the strip of desert between San Francisco and New Reno**. Tough
aliens, deathclaws, floaters and centaurs all pay well — centaurs are the easiest. Avoid fire geckos.

:::note
Encounter size scales with your party. Enclave patrols are three soldiers for a party of 1–3 and up to
**seven** for a full group.

So if you want big Enclave patrols but do not want your good companions taking damage, bring one
**disposable** companion instead of none.
:::

## Kaga

:::note[An RPU addition]
Kaga was Arroyo's greatest warrior until outside influences turned him. He cheated in the Temple of
Trials, was cast out, and now carries a grudge against you.
<Vanilla>Kaga does not exist.</Vanilla>
:::

Ask **Mynoc** in Arroyo about him for the full story.

He ambushes you **five times** over the course of the game, better equipped each time. The first time
he talks first; after that he attacks on sight.

| Encounter | Requirement | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | — | Ocean–Arroyo–Klamath, and the Klamath–Den route |
| 2nd | Level 5+ | Bandits Pass and around Modoc |
| 3rd | Level 9+ | Raider patrol areas |
| 4th | Level 13+ | Around NCR |
| 5th | Level 17+ | Around San Francisco and the Redding trade route |

Hurt him badly and he tries to escape while his companions stay and fight. Kill him and the encounters
stop.

:::caution
Kaga's escape is unreliable. Sometimes he starts to run and then simply stands still, ignoring your
shots. End combat and he leaves properly. If it happens, decide whether you want more encounters
later — if so, leave him alone for the rest of the fight.
:::

## Special encounters

One-time encounters, usually with a joke attached. Some leave a permanent green circle on the world
map (marked "Unknown" until you enter); others flash red and leave nothing.

**All special encounters occur in desert terrain**, except the Bridge, which is a mountain encounter.
Coast and city terrain have none. The **Bridge has the highest probability**, so it is usually your
first.

Minimum level is given in parentheses.

:::danger
The **Guardian Portal** and all three bad encounters **reset every time you enter**. Anything dropped
there is lost.

You cannot leave the car on a special encounter map unless it is out of fuel.
:::

### The good ones

**A Guardian Portal (8)** — a large stone ring; a *Star Trek* reference. Walk in and you arrive on
Vault 13 level 3, eighty years ago. Loot the lockers and chests. Break the beeping water chip computer
for **1000 XP** and return.
<Vanilla>The Solar Scorcher is on the armoury floor here. RPU moves it to the EPA.</Vanilla>

**A man guarding a bridge (10)** — a Monty Python reference, and the exit grid (and your car) are on
the far side. He asks two straightforward questions and one game trivia question.

- **Answer wrong and you die.**
- Answer all three correctly and he grumbles and leaves.
- **Answer the final question with a counter-question** and he dies instead: **500 XP**.

Take the **Bridgekeeper's Robes** — identical to Combat Armor but lighter, with no radiation
protection, and they look ridiculous.

:::danger
Attack him and he summons an endless swarm of **exploding brahmin**. Killing him is worth **7500 XP**
but he is nearly impervious — only criticals hurt him.

He only produces a new cow when an existing one dies, so you can enter combat mode and ignore the
non-exploding cows while you work on him.

**Do not kill him with a 0-damage instant-death critical** — on the original engine the kill fails to
register and you get no XP. The **Living Anatomy** perk adds 5 damage after resistance and makes
0-damage kills impossible, which sidesteps it entirely.

*Not confirmed on FOR:CE.* Living Anatomy is worth taking regardless, so the advice is unchanged.
:::

**A giant stone head (10)** — the Sacred Head of the Vault Dweller. Listen for a while and you receive
a **Monument Chunk**: ST +3, AG +3, DR +50, temporarily. Rest 12 hours to skip to the end of the
monologue.

:::danger
You can **Steal** three more chunks — but only with Steal **tagged or at 95%+**. Below that you are
**instantly killed**, even if you take nothing.
:::

**A tin woodsman (6, red)** — a man in power armour who cannot move. Use the oil can next to him on
him: **150 Micro Fusion Cells**.

**The Cafe of Broken Dreams (1)** — an uncannily familiar layout. Talk to everyone. **Dogmeat** joins
if you remove your armour so the vault suit shows (or wear the Bridgekeeper's Robes, which is a bug).

:::caution
You can also recruit Dogmeat with an **iguana-on-a-stick**, one of which is lying nearby. This **crashes
the game** most of the time. Use the vault suit.

This encounter only appears in the **central and southern** parts of the world map.
:::

**The Unwashed Villagers hunting a spammer (6, red)** — an old Fallout community in-joke. They carry
about **600 rounds of 10mm JHP** between them, and there are **6 stimpaks and a fruit** in a nearby
locker.

**A crashed shuttle (6)** — the USS Torres. Search the bodies for **3 hypos**, each restoring 75–100 HP.
<Vanilla>RPU adds a **phaser pistol** here: small energy cells, 12-shot clip, 20–30 damage, range
25.</Vanilla>

**King Arthur's Knights (10, red)** — heavily armed Brotherhood warriors looking for the Holy Hand
Grenade of Antioch. Say you have seen it and point in a random direction for **$500**. Then ask about
the G.E.C.K. and they point similarly. Killing them is hard and yields only a Gatling Laser.

**The Vorpal Rat Cave** — RPU adds this as a follow-up to King Arthur's Knights.
<Vanilla>The cave does not exist, and the Holy Hand Grenade is not obtainable.</Vanilla>

The five knights fight a seemingly invulnerable rat, and neither side cares about you as long as you
stay out of it. The fight is long and can end either way.

:::tip
Use your action points **during the battle** to slip into the cave and take the **Holy Hand Grenade**.

More practically: each knight carries **$500 and over 100 large energy cells**, depending on how much
ammunition they have used. Loot or steal accordingly.
:::

### The pointless one

**The remains of a whale (6)** — a *Hitchhiker's Guide* reference, complete with a pot of daisies.
The only encounter with no effect whatsoever.

### The bad ones

**A lone surviving dog (6)** — the **pariah dog**. It follows you if you fail a Luck check, which
repeats every 10 seconds. It gives you the **Jinxed** perk, **drops your Luck to 1**, and occupies a
companion slot. It has **750 HP** and the only way to be rid of it is to kill it.

:::tip[Killing it cheaply]
Take it to **Sierra Army Depot level 2** and stand it on the electric floor. Or, if you are evil, use
the **organ extractor** on it twice: 2 human brains and **−200 karma**.

Killing it restores your Luck and removes Jinxed — and the dog reappears on the Pariahs map, which is
probably a bug.
:::

:::note[Your Luck comes back exactly as it was]
The dog drops your Luck to **1** (**2** with Gifted) however you got there, and killing it restores the
base and bonus parts to precisely their previous values. There is no way to end up ahead.
<Vanilla>Luck raised above its starting value before the dog joined did not drop at all, and killing
the dog then *added* your current Luck again — LK 4 became 7, and 6 or more became 10.</Vanilla>

Using Mutate! to acquire Gifted does not trigger the drop.
:::

**Toxic waste dump (6)** — green radioactive goo everywhere, geckos of every kind, and a massive
radiation dose before anything even breathes fire at you. The exit grid is on the right.

**A herd of brahmin (6)** — they walk towards you and explode, and new ones materialise. Leave before
your companions start dying.

## The Mysterious Stranger

If you took the perk, the chance of them appearing in an encounter is `30 + LK×2` out of 100. Their HP,
armour and weapon scale with your level.

:::tip[Infinite ammunition]
Enter an empty spot on the world map and the stranger may be standing nearby. **Steal** their ammunition
and stimpaks — getting caught does nothing.

Then enter combat, spend a round running around, and end combat. As they walk away, **save and reload**
and they stop. Steal again for fresh ammunition. If they escape, leave the map and repeat.

On some mountain maps the stranger fails to leave properly, so you can steal repeatedly without any of
this. Micro Fusion Cells and 4.7mm Caseless in quantity.
:::

:::note
RPU includes an **Improved Mysterious Stranger** mod, so the perk is less useless than it was.
<Vanilla>The Stranger behaves erratically and is generally considered a wasted perk.</Vanilla>
:::
