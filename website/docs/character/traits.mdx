# Traits

You may take up to **two** traits, or none. Each is a permanent trade-off. Only the
**Mutate!** perk can change one later, and it costs a perk slot to do so.

## The good ones

### Gifted
**+1 to every SPECIAL, −10% to all skills, −5 skill points per level.**

Universally regarded as the strongest trait in the game, and it is hard to argue otherwise.
Seven stat points is enormous value; the skill penalty is largely offset by the extra
Intelligence, and you will have more skill points than uses for them by the midgame anyway.

The only real argument against it is flavour — it makes the character feel less like a
choice and more like a default. If you were going to put Intelligence at 10 regardless, the
skill point loss can't be recouped, which slightly dulls the appeal.

**Take it** unless you specifically want the challenge of building without it.

### Small Frame
**+1 Agility, carry weight reduced (ST × 15 instead of ST × 25).**

An extra character point in the stat that is hardest to raise later. The carry weight hurts
for the first few hours and then stops mattering: you get Sulik almost immediately, then more
companions, then the car, then Power Armor.

**Take it.** Pair it with ST 5–6 and you will barely notice.

### Finesse
**+10% critical chance, −30% damage per hit.**

A solid early-game trait — critical hits are how you kill things efficiently before you have
good weapons. It becomes redundant once you have **Sniper** (with decent Luck) or **Slayer**,
at which point the damage penalty is pure loss.

That makes it a natural candidate for the **Mutate!** swap: run Finesse to level 24, take
Sniper, then Mutate! it into Fast Shot.

### Fast Shot
**−1 AP cost on all ranged attacks, cannot make targeted shots.**

Excellent for anyone using burst weapons or firing many shots per round, where targeted
shots were never the plan. Combined with Bonus Rate of Fire you can empty a pistol five or
six times a round.

:::caution
Fast Shot does **not** reduce AP cost for hand-to-hand attacks, but it *does* remove your
ability to make targeted blows. It is actively bad for melee and unarmed characters — a
change from Fallout 1, where it behaved sensibly.
:::

Do not take it if you intend to be a dedicated eye-shot sniper.

### One Hander
**+20% accuracy with one-handed weapons, −40% with two-handed.**

Awkward for gun characters, because most of the best late weapons are two-handed. Very good
for unarmed characters — every Unarmed weapon (Power Fist, Mega Power Fist, brass knuckles)
counts as one-handed, so it is a straight +20%. Your bare fists and kicks are unaffected
either way.

## Situational

### Good Natured
**+15% First Aid, Doctor, Speech, Barter; −10% Small Guns, Big Guns, Energy Weapons,
Unarmed, Melee, Throwing.**

Effectively a skill point trade that favours you — the skills it raises are more useful than
the ones it lowers, and your combat skill will exceed 100% regardless. Essential for a pure
diplomat. Marginal for everyone else.

### Sex Appeal
**+ reaction from the opposite sex, − from the same.**

More useful for female characters, since most shopkeepers and quest-givers in the wasteland
are men. It has a visible effect on barter prices if your Charisma is low, and unlocks a
handful of alternate dialogue solutions. No effect at CH 10 (positive) or CH 1 (negative).

Take it if you want it. It will not carry a build.

### Heavy Handed
**+4 melee damage, −30% critical damage.**

Genuinely helpful in the first few hours, when four extra damage per punch is a large
fraction of the total. Actively harmful late, when your criticals are doing the heavy lifting.

### Jinxed
**Everyone — you, allies and enemies — fails critically far more often.**

Chaos. Defensible for a pure unarmed character who wants enemies fumbling their guns, and it
pairs infamously with HtH Evade. If you or your companions rely on firearms, you are
randomising fights you were already winning.

## Avoid

### Skilled
**+5 skill points per level, one fewer perk every three levels.**

The trap trait. You will not run out of skill points, and you will run out of perks. Over a
long game this is roughly 60 extra skill points per perk given up — a bad rate when perks
like Sniper and Bonus Rate of Fire exist.

### Bruiser
**+2 Strength, −2 Action Points.**

Two AP is roughly equivalent to four points of Agility. Catastrophic.

### Fast Metabolism
**+2 healing rate, no radiation or poison resistance.**

Both halves are near-invisible. Healing rate is never the problem; you rest to heal.

### Kamikaze
**+5 Sequence, Armor Class 0 when unarmoured.**

Trades your protection for the privilege of acting first in round one.

### Bloody Mess
**Deaths always show the most violent animation.**

No mechanical effect at all. Amusing for an hour, then merely slow.

### Chem Reliant / Chem Resistant
**Faster addiction and withdrawal / half addiction chance but half duration.**

Chems are a minor part of Fallout 2. Neither trait changes enough to notice, and most
players reload if they get addicted anyway.

:::note
RPU adds two new addictions the original game defined but never implemented: **alcohol** and
the card game **Tragic**. Neither is dangerous, but they exist now.
<Vanilla>Neither addiction can be acquired in the unmodded game.</Vanilla>
:::

## Recommended pairings

| Build | Traits |
| --- | --- |
| General-purpose gunner | Gifted + Small Frame |
| Sniper | Gifted + Finesse (Mutate! to Fast Shot at 24) |
| Brawler / unarmed | Gifted + One Hander |
| Diplomat | Gifted + Good Natured |
| No-Gifted challenge | Small Frame + Fast Shot |
