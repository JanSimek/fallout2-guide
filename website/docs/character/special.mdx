# SPECIAL

You start with 5 in every stat and 5 points to distribute. You can also lower a stat to
free up points. Traits can shift things further — Gifted adds one to everything, Small Frame
and Bruiser trade one thing for another.

:::tip
If a trait raises a stat, you can redistribute the gained point manually on the creation
screen. Treat Gifted, Small Frame and Bruiser as sources of generic character points.
:::

## Strength

**Governs:** carry weight, melee damage, weapon requirements.

Fallout 2 is a game about hauling loot to shops. Strength is how much of it you can carry
at once, and for a good stretch of the early game that is genuinely the constraint on your
income.

**Start with 5.** That is enough for nearly every early weapon and a good many late ones —
the H&K P90c, the Gauss Pistol and the Pulse Rifle all sit at ST 4–5. ST 6 covers the Gauss
Rifle and the Bozar; ST 7 covers miniguns. But Power Armor gives +3 (Advanced +4), so by the
time you actually want a minigun you will have the Strength for it regardless.

Going above 6 at creation is close to a waste. Falling below 5 is painful early.

:::note
Using a weapon below its Strength requirement is a **−20% accuracy penalty per point**, not
a hard block. Wielding a Bozar at ST 5 costs you 20% to hit, which a few dozen skill points
will cover — often a better trade than a character point.
:::

## Perception

**Governs:** ranged accuracy at distance, Awareness perk, some dialogue and observation checks.

**Start with 5 minimum, 6 preferred.** Five is the requirement for **Awareness**, which
should be your first perk and which you will use constantly for the rest of the game. Six
opens a few additional checks — notably spotting the rebranded brahmin in Klamath — and is
worth the point even for a melee character.

Raiseable by 2 during play.

## Endurance

**Governs:** Hit Points, healing rate, poison and radiation resistance.

The weakest stat in the game, and the easiest place to find spare points. HP per level is
`EN/2 + 2`, rounded down, so two extra points of Endurance buys you roughly 30 HP over an
entire playthrough. The Lifegiver perk gives more than that for a single perk slot.

**Start with 4**, or 3 if you need the point elsewhere. Melee characters who plan to be hit
a lot can justify 5–6.

Raiseable by only 1 during play.

## Charisma

**Governs:** party size, barter prices, a number of dialogue options.

Your maximum number of companions is **Charisma ÷ 2, rounded down**. This is the main reason
to care about it.

| Charisma | Companions |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 0 |
| 2–3 | 1 |
| 4–5 | 2 |
| 6–7 | 3 |
| 8–9 | 4 |
| 10 | 5 |

**Start with at least 2** even if you plan to solo, so you can pick up a companion
temporarily for the experience. **4 is comfortable** — two companions is enough to carry
loot and add firepower without turning every fight into a five-minute AI turn.

:::tip[Recruit while boosted]
Party size is checked at recruitment only. Eat Mentats (+1 CH each, capped at +2), recruit
your companion, and they stay when the drug wears off. The exception is Lenny, who will
leave a CH 1 character.
:::

Raiseable by 3 during play (Gain Charisma perk, Vault City surgery, Mirrored Shades).

## Intelligence

**Governs:** skill points per level, and a very large number of dialogue options.

**The most important stat in the game.** Skill points per level are `IN × 2 + 5`, and more
importantly, low Intelligence progressively closes off conversation. Many quests simply
cannot be started by a dim character.

**Start with 7 at minimum, 8 is the sweet spot.** At 7 you occasionally need a Mentat for a
check that wants 8 or 9; at 8 that is rare. Above 9 the returns are thin.

Below 4, your character speaks only in grunts. This is a legitimate — and very funny — way
to play, with a substantial amount of unique dialogue written for it. See
[Low Intelligence](/systems/low-intelligence).

:::note
Mentats work in reverse too. If you want to see an Intelligence-under-4 response during a
normal game, take a couple of Mentats and wait for the crash — your stats dip below normal
for a while. Psycho is even more effective.
:::

Raiseable by 3 during play.

## Agility

**Governs:** Action Points, Armor Class, Sequence.

Action Points are `AG ÷ 2 + 5`. Every combat round is spent from that pool, so Agility is
effectively "how much of the game you get to play".

**Start with 6 minimum, 8 if you can afford it.** Below 6 combat becomes genuinely tedious.

Only raiseable by 1 during play, which makes it the most expensive stat to fix later. Get it
right at creation.

:::tip[Odd numbers]
Because AP is calculated with integer division, AG 6 and AG 7 both give 8 AP. An odd starting
Agility is not wasted, though — it turns the Gain Agility perk into an effective extra Action
Boy. The same logic applies to Endurance and Charisma.
:::

## Luck

**Governs:** critical hit chance, special encounter frequency, various hidden rolls.

Easy to dismiss, and for the first half of the game you can. It becomes significant if you
take the **Sniper** perk, which rolls against Luck for a critical on every hit — at LK 10
with Sniper you crit essentially every time.

**Start with 5–6.** Take it higher only if you are building around Sniper. Raiseable by up
to 3 during play, including from the Hubologist Zeta Scan and the "weird brain alignment"
encounters.

:::caution
The two "weird alignment" outcomes — one giving IN +1 and LK +1, the other LK +2 — are
mutually exclusive. You get one or the other, not both.
:::
