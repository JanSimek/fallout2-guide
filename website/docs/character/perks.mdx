# Perks

You get a perk every **three levels** (every four if you took Skilled). You must spend each
one before you can earn the next, so a perk you skip is a perk you lose.

Plan ahead. Some perks have stat requirements you will only meet after taking a *different*
perk, and some can be reached by drinking a chem at the right moment — though **Luck
requirements cannot be met with drugs**.

## The three that matter most

### Awareness
*Level 3, PE 5*

Shows every target's exact hit points, weapon and ammunition when you hover over them. You
will use it in every single fight for the rest of the game, and it turns "is this winnable?"
from a guess into a readout.

**Take it at level 3.** This is the reason to start with PE 5.

### Bonus Rate of Fire
*Level 15, AG 7, PE 6, IN 4*

−1 AP on every ranged attack. Stacks with Fast Shot. This is the difference between three
shots a round and five.

### Sniper
*Level 24, AG 8, PE 8, LK 6*

Rolls your Luck for a critical hit on **every** successful ranged attack. It does not replace
your normal critical chance — it adds an independent second chance on top.

The practical effect is dramatic and scales hard with Luck:

| Luck | Crit chance without Sniper | With Sniper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 4% | ~42% |
| 6 | 6% | ~64% |
| 8 | 8% | ~82% |
| 10 | 10% | ~100% |

:::note
Aimed shots still help after Sniper — an eye shot adds +60% to the *normal* roll — but the
marginal benefit shrinks a lot at high Luck (roughly +12% at LK 8, +6% at LK 9). At that
point Fast Shot's extra attack is usually worth more. Conversely, **More Criticals becomes
nearly worthless once you have Sniper** with decent Luck.
:::

Melee equivalent: **Slayer** (level 24, AG 8, ST 8, Unarmed 80%) turns every hand-to-hand hit
into a critical with no Luck roll at all.

## Strongly recommended

| Perk | Level | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Better Criticals** | 9 | +20% on the critical effect table. More crippling, more damage multipliers. |
| **Action Boy** (×2) | 12 | +1 AP each. Aim for a total AP that is an exact multiple of your weapon's attack cost, plus reload. |
| **Living Anatomy** | 12 | +5 damage against all living targets, plus 10% Doctor. Quietly excellent. |
| **Bonus HtH Attacks** | 15 | −1 AP per melee/unarmed attack. Essential for brawlers. |
| **Bonus Move** (×2) | 6 | +2 free movement AP per rank. Superb for melee, and useful for gunners repositioning around companions. |
| **Quick Pockets** | 15 | Inventory access for 2 AP instead of 4. A convenience that becomes a lifesaver when you need a Stimpak mid-fight. |
| **Gain Agility** | 12 | Only if it takes you to an *even* Agility, which means a real extra Action Point. |

:::note
**Bonus Move is bugged**: saving and reloading during combat restores the extra movement AP,
letting you cross any distance in one round.
:::

## Worth considering

- **Dodger** (+5 AC) and **HtH Evade** — HtH Evade is strictly better for unarmed characters,
  and unlike the description implies, it works while holding Unarmed-class weapons such as
  the Power Fist. It only needs your *weapon* slots free of guns.
- **More Criticals** (×3) — good until you get Sniper, then redundant.
- **Toughness** (×3) — +10% damage resistance each. Damage resistance caps at 90% and you can
  reach 80% through armour alone, so more than one rank often wastes itself.
- **Lifegiver** (×2) — +4 max HP per level retroactively. Up to ~140 extra HP by level 30.
  Better than putting character points into Endurance, still not a top pick.
- **Gain Charisma / Gain Intelligence / Gain Luck / Gain Perception** — all situational.
  Gain Charisma is worth it if it takes you to an even number (one more companion). Gain Luck
  is genuinely strong if you have Sniper.
- **Magnetic Personality** — +1 effective Charisma for party size only. If your Charisma is
  odd, Gain Charisma is strictly better. Available from level 6, not level 3 as the manual claims.
- **Mutate!** — swap one trait. The canonical use is trading **Finesse for Fast Shot** at
  level 27 (or 21), just as Sniper makes Finesse redundant and removes the reason to aim.
- **Explorer / Ranger / Scout** — better special encounter odds. Late-game fun, not a priority.
- **Silent Death** — double damage sneaking up behind an enemy. Six conditions for a 2× multiplier.

:::caution[Cult of Personality and Karma Beacon work now]
Both perks are fully implemented in RPU, and **Cult of Personality** in particular has real
consequences: it lets you take slaver-aligned quests (such as Vortis's job against the NCR
Ranger safe houses) without tanking your karma.
<Vanilla>In the unmodded game both perks are inert — they do literally nothing.</Vanilla>
:::

## Do not take these

**Skill point perks** — Educated, Gambler, Harmless, Master Thief, Medic, Mr. Fixit,
Negotiator, Salesman, Speaker, Survivalist, Comprehension. You will not run short of skill
points. Spending a perk to get some is spending your most limited resource for your least.

The one arguable exception is **Tag!**, because of a quirk: tagging a skill raises it by the
same amount you had already raised it, and you can then *lower* it again before leaving the
character screen, banking the difference as generic points. Tag a skill sitting at 299% and
you get several hundred points. It is an exploit, and it is still only skill points.

**Others to skip:**

- **Swift Learner** (×3) and **Here and Now** — perks that help you get perks. The XP curve is
  linear, so three ranks of Swift Learner leaves you about two levels ahead by level 30.
- **Sharpshooter** — a real **+2 Perception** per rank for range calculations. Still a thin
  effect compared with simply raising your weapon skill, but no longer broken.
  <Vanilla>Bugged: it delivers less than +1 PE would, because the range calculation uses PE×2
  and the perk only compensates for PE.</Vanilla>
- **Pathfinder** (×2) — **25% less world map travel time per rank**, and both ranks work. Time
  is still not a constraint in Fallout 2, so it remains a weak pick — but on its merits, not
  because it is broken.
  <Vanilla>Bugged: rank 1 gives 20% instead of 25%, and rank 2 does nothing at all.</Vanilla>
- **Light Step** — avoids floor traps, of which there are essentially two sets in the game
  (Redding and outside the SAD), and it still makes you roll Agility for each.
- **Mysterious Stranger** — RPU improves the Stranger's behaviour, but the perk still asks
  you to give up a slot for an occasional uncontrolled ally.
- **Bonus HtH Damage** (×3) — adds to *maximum* damage only, so about +1 average per rank.
- **Bonus Ranged Damage** (×2) — too small unless you are firing a Bozar or minigun.
- **Adrenaline Rush, Cautious Nature, Demolition Expert, Earlier Sequence, Empathy,
  Faster Healing, Fortune Finder, Gain Endurance, Gain Strength, Ghost, Harmless, Heave Ho!,
  Kama Sutra Master, Master Trader, Night Vision, Pack Rat, Presence, Pickpocket, Pyromaniac,
  Quick Recovery, Rad Resistance, Silent Running, Smooth Talker, Snakeater, Stonewall,
  Strong Back, Weapon Handling** — all marginal, situational, or both.

:::note
**Animal Friend, Flower Child, Mental Block and Scrounger** exist in the game files but
cannot be obtained. They are Fallout 1 leftovers.
:::

## A workable pick order

For the general-purpose gunner from [Suggested Builds](/character/builds):

| Level | Perk |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | Awareness |
| 6 | Bonus Move *(or Toughness)* |
| 9 | Better Criticals |
| 12 | Living Anatomy |
| 15 | Bonus Rate of Fire |
| 18 | Action Boy |
| 21 | Action Boy |
| 24 | **Sniper** |
| 27 | Mutate! → Fast Shot *(if you took Finesse)* |
| 30 | Gain Luck |

For a brawler, replace Bonus Rate of Fire with **Bonus HtH Attacks** and Sniper with
**Slayer**, and take **HtH Evade** early.
