# Levelling and Experience

## The curve

Experience requirements rise linearly: each level needs progressively more than the last, in
a steady step rather than a doubling. This is why **Swift Learner** and **Here and Now** are
poor perks — you cannot outrun a linear curve by spending the resource the curve exists to
give you.

A thorough playthrough that does most of the content lands somewhere around **level 25–30**
by the Oil Rig. You do not need to grind.

## Level cap

The cap is **99**, and each level costs `level × (level − 1) ÷ 2 × 1000` XP — so the last few are:

| Level | XP required |
| --- | --- |
| 97 | 4,656,000 |
| 98 | 4,753,000 |
| 99 | **4,851,000** |

You will not get near this by playing normally. Getting there means deliberately farming random
encounters for a very long time.

:::note[On the original engine, 98 skipped straight to 99]
Reaching 4,753,000 XP on the 1998 executable awarded **two** levels at once, jumping from 97 to
99 and skipping 98 entirely.

FOR:CE gets this right — it walks the level up one step at a time and checks each threshold, so
98 costs 4,753,000 and 99 costs 4,851,000.
<Vanilla>Level 98 is unreachable: the moment you qualify for it you are given level 99 as
well.</Vanilla>
:::

## The time limit

The game ends after **13 years** of in-game time, which lands on 25 July 2254. A screen
saying "the end" appears and that is that.

This is not a real constraint. A completionist run uses three or four years at most.

:::caution[The other deadlines are real]
The 13-year limit is generous, but several *quests* have tight timers — the Vault City
village mole rat job expires after a month, the Arroyo GECK deadline drives the plot, and a
few New Reno jobs go stale. Those are flagged in the walkthrough where they apply.
:::

## Where the experience is

Roughly, in descending order of value:

1. **Quest completion**, especially peaceful solutions. RPU pays notably better for
   non-violent outcomes — 750 XP for talking the Den squatters out of the building versus
   500 for killing them, 1000 for arranging their employment.
2. **Killing things.** Reliable, slow, and it competes with quest XP in places where killing
   the quest-giver forfeits the reward.
3. **Skill checks in dialogue and on objects** — hacking terminals, disarming traps,
   repairing machinery. Small individually, substantial in aggregate.
4. **Discovering locations.** Every new map on the world map pays out.

## Unspent skill points

Capped at **99** when you level up. You can hold more than 99 temporarily — from a skill
point perk, for instance — but the excess is discarded at your next level. Spend before you
level.

## A note on exploits

Fallout 2 is old and leaky, and older guides list a set of well-known tricks. **Most of them no
longer work**, because FOR:CE fixes them by default.

| Trick | On FOR:CE |
| --- | --- |
| Number key on a town map to enter an area you have not unlocked | **Blocked.** `town_map_hotkeys_fix` |
| Press `0` to abort any conversation | **Blocked.** `no_exit_hotkey` |
| Save and reload mid-combat to refill **Bonus Move** AP | **Still works** |
| The **Tag!** skill-point trick | **Still works** |

The first two are switches in `<DAT>/config/game.cfg`, so you can turn them back on if you want
the original behaviour. Both are on — meaning fixed — out of the box.

:::note[Why Bonus Move still works]
Loading a save during combat deliberately restarts your turn so the fight can resume, and
restarting a turn is what refills free move AP. It is a consequence of handling the reload
sanely rather than an oversight, so it is unlikely to change.
<Vanilla>Identical behaviour — this one is inherited, not introduced.</Vanilla>
:::

Several of the tricks that *do* still work can corrupt a quest state or drop you inside a wall.
The guide mentions them where they genuinely help you recover from a stuck situation, and
otherwise leaves them alone.
