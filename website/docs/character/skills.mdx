# Skills

You tag **three** skills at creation. Tagged skills start 20% higher and rise at double rate
per skill point spent — which is to say, tagging a skill halves its lifetime cost.

The Tag! perk can add a fourth later.

## What to tag

### Small Guns
Your combat skill for roughly the first two-thirds of the game, and a perfectly viable one
for all of it. Pistols and rifles are what you will find, what you can afford ammunition
for, and what companions can use.

Tag it unless you are deliberately building around melee, unarmed or energy weapons.

### Speech
The single most quest-relevant skill in the game. Dozens of quests have a peaceful, better-
paying solution locked behind a Speech check, and RPU adds more of them — several of the new
quests in San Francisco and NCR are gated at 80–100%.

Tag it. Get it to 100% early and keep pushing.

### Lockpick
There are a great many locked doors and containers, and most of them have something worth
having behind them. Lockpick also has the pleasant property that failure is usually
retryable — until you critically fail and jam the lock permanently.

Tag it, or accept that you will walk past a lot of loot.

## Melee alternatives

If you are building a brawler, swap out Small Guns:

### Melee Weapons
Only worth tagging if it is your primary. If it is, tag it and rush it to 100% — early melee
is unforgiving and a Super Sledge later is genuinely strong.

### Unarmed
Rises to roughly 100% for free through the Chosen One's natural progression and a few
in-game boosts, so tagging it is arguably a waste. If you intend to go unarmed all the way,
tag it anyway — the difference between 100% and 200% is real, and HtH Evade scales with it.

## Worth points, not a tag

| Skill | Target | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Doctor** | 75%+ | Heals crippled limbs, which First Aid cannot. RPU makes this matter — see below. |
| **Science** | 101%, then 110%+ | Many checks. The Shi terminals in San Francisco want **110%** to hack cleanly. |
| **Big Guns** | as needed | Late-game only. Do not invest until you own a minigun. |
| **Energy Weapons** | as needed | Same. |
| **Repair** | 100% | Several RPU checks in the Shi submarine roll against it. Raise with books first. |
| **Traps** | 60–70% | Few traps in the game, but RPU adds trap-based options — Vic's door in Klamath, planting explosives on the submarine. |

:::caution Doctor matters more in RPU
RPU lets you and your companions suffer **crippled limbs** from the Modoc outhouse explosion
and the Ghost Farm trapdoor. Crippled limbs do not heal with rest or First Aid — you need
Doctor, a Doctor's Bag, or a town doctor. Watch for the "Crippled" and "NPC Crippled"
on-screen messages.
<Vanilla>In the unmodded game these two events cannot cripple anyone.</Vanilla>
:::

## Raise with books, not points

These four cap out at **91%** from books but that is more than enough:

- **First Aid** — does almost nothing Doctor does not, apart from a free +20% to its own roll.
- **Repair** — *Dean's Electronics*
- **Science** — *Big Book of Science*
- **Outdoorsman** — *Scout Handbook*, and it mostly just reduces random encounters.

Books stop working at 91%. Read them before you spend points, not after.

:::tip
Skill point cost is calculated from your *current* level, and the Game Difficulty setting
shifts that level. Set difficulty to Hard before spending points on a skill sitting just
above 100%, and each point costs less. Books can be exploited the same way.
:::

## Skills to skip

### Barter and Gambling
Money stops being a constraint by the midgame, and Charisma affects prices more than Barter
does below 100%. Do not spend a single point here.

### Throwing
Grenades and knives are a footnote in Fallout 2. There is not enough throwable ordnance in
the world to build around.

### Sneak
Defensible, not recommended. It lets you set up assassinations and close distance before a
fight, and it substantially improves your odds when planting explosives. It does almost
nothing for stealing — most shop scripts ignore sneak entirely.

### Steal
A save-scumming skill. You can steal successfully at low skill if you reload on failure, and
if you do not reload, being caught turns whole towns hostile. Its main legitimate use is
**looking** at what people are carrying, which works at any skill level.

:::note Stealing details worth knowing
- You get a bonus from behind or the side. Sneak does not help.
- Item *size* matters unless you have the Pickpocket perk.
- It works on desks and workbenches but not on lockers or footlockers.
- Consecutive successful steals award escalating XP (10, then 20, then 30…), capped at
  `300 − your Steal skill`. Being caught forfeits all of it.
- Some merchants keep stock off-screen. Put an item in the barter window and back out — it
  often becomes visible to the Steal interface.
:::

## How high is high enough?

**101% is the practical ceiling for most skills.** Almost every check in the game is at or
below it, and most can simply be retried on failure.

Exceptions:

- **Combat skills** keep paying until you hit 95% to-hit against everything at any sensible
  range — higher still if you make aimed shots.
- **Science** has RPU checks at 110%.
- **Lockpick** has RPU checks around 100–120% (the Hubologist stash safes).
- **Unarmed** scales HtH Evade indefinitely.
- **Speech** has RPU checks at 96–101%.

:::note
Unspent skill points are capped at 99 when you level up. You can hold more than 99 — from a
skill point perk, say — but only until your next level, at which point the excess vanishes.
:::
