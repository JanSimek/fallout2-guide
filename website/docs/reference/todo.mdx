# To Check

Most of this guide is checked against the game's own files: RPU's scripts and dialogue, the item and
critter protos, and the [FOR:CE](https://github.com/fallout2-ce/fallout2-ce) engine source. This page
is the other part — what has **not** been settled, kept in the open rather than quietly asserted.

Two kinds of thing are listed:

- **Marked claims.** Anywhere you see the <Unverified>Like this one: an icon in the text, with a note
  saying what could not be confirmed.</Unverified> icon, the claim is on this list. Most are things a
  script cannot decide — how the engine behaves at runtime, or whether a bug still bites.
- **Open work**, below: whole areas that have not had the same treatment yet.

If you settle one at the keyboard, the fix is usually a one-line change on the page it came from.

## Open work

### Directions that depend on where something sits on a map

The guide now describes **RPU 2.4.x** throughout. Going from 2.3.34 to 2.4.34 changes 2042 files,
but only 44 of them carry text or script: the rest is Pixote's remade map art, and not one critter
or item proto moves, so no stat, XP, damage or price figure in the guide is affected. Of the 44,
three facts reached these pages and all three have been fixed.

**What needs doing:** the remaining risk is not numbers but *directions* — "north of the white
shed", "the locker on the left". Those were written against the old maps, and the EPA storage room
turned out to be one of them.

The scope is now measured rather than guessed. 170 of the 176 maps changed, but comparing the two
releases object by object shows what that change *is*: scenery is cut by 10–40% on every town map,
while **critters, Misc and containers stay put**. Re-tiling, not re-planning. So a direction that
points at a person or a container is almost always safe, and one that points at decorative
scenery — a shed, a barrel, a parked truck — is the kind that rots.

Checked and clear:

| Page | Result |
| --- | --- |
| The Den | every critter identical across `denbus1`, `denbus2`, `denres1`; gravesites identical |
| Broken Hills | every critter identical across `broken1`, `broken2` |
| Redding | one Gun Guard moved a hex in `reddown`; nothing the guide mentions |
| EPA | the one page that did drift — see the plant counts, now fixed |

The item counts move by one or two on three maps (a Locker, Tool Board and Workbench gone from
Broken Hills downtown, a Bookcase from the residential map, a Knife added in the Den), and none is
a container the guide sends you to.

**Still to do:** the 85 remaining directional claims on the other pages, by the same method — diff
the two releases for that town's maps first, and only read the claims whose landmark actually
moved. `mounts` must report `rpuVersion: v2.4.34` before any of it counts.

### Pages checked only where they mention companions

The walkthrough was corrected wherever a companion was involved, which is not the same as being
checked. Quest steps, XP figures, skill checks and directions that never mention a companion have not
been read against the scripts.

**What needs doing:** a page-by-page pass, in the order the walkthrough runs. `check-rpu-drift.py`
already catches quests and XP figures that no script awards; the rest is reading.

### Engine claims inherited from the 1998 executable

The guide inherited a set of bugs and tricks from Per Jorner's guide, written for the original
executable. Many are fixed in FOR:CE, and the audited ones say so — see
[Known Issues](/reference/known-issues). The rest are neither confirmed nor ruled out, because they
depend on runtime behaviour that reading the source does not settle; they are in the list below,
alongside everything else still open.

**What needs doing:** playing. Each of these is a save, a reload and five minutes.

### Nothing here has been verified in play

Every fact in this guide comes from reading the game's files and the engine's source. That is a good
way to get numbers and conditions right, and a poor way to notice that something does not work the
way the code implies. Where those two can disagree, the claim is marked.

### [San Francisco](/walkthrough/san-francisco)

- [ ] [Always use **end turn**, never end combat.](/walkthrough/san-francisco#martial-arts-training) *(Martial arts training)*<br />
  **Why it is unconfirmed:** The window is narrow and specific. `fcdragon.ssl` notices you have won inside `combat_p_proc`, which only runs while combat is running: when the opponent drops below a quarter of their hit points it arms a timer, and that timer is the only thing that ever gives your inventory back, heals you and moves you out of the ring — during a test `ReturnInven` is reached only from `endTestPass` and `endTestFail`, and nothing but those timers calls either. Killing outright is covered rather than missed: a dead, prone or knocked-out opponent is scored as 0 hit points out of 4, so it counts as below the quarter and arms the timer exactly like a blow that leaves them standing. What is at risk is not the kill but the Dragon's next turn, since his is the `combat_p_proc` doing the looking. If combat is still going when the timer fires it calls `terminate_combat` and re-arms itself, so ordinarily it cannot lose you — but end combat by hand before the opponent's health is ever checked and no timer is armed at all, and nothing is left to restore you. That is the shape of the reported bug, and it is consistent with it. Still not reproduced, though the ring fight itself has now been played: the Dragon will only teach you at 250 karma or better, and a test is a separate dialogue branch from the lessons. Through it the ordinary path is sturdy — the timer arms, the next opponent is fetched, the fight resumes on the ring tile. The end-combat attempt is simply refused throughout, since Fallout 2 binds it to the same key that declines to end a fight anyone is still engaged in, and in the ring the Dragon's men and the Chinatown crowd are all in combat. Whether a quieter ring lets the key through, and with it the window above, is the part still open.
