# To Check

Most of this guide is checked against the game's own files: RPU's scripts and dialogue, the item and
critter protos, and the [FOR:CE](https://github.com/fallout2-ce/fallout2-ce) engine source. This page
is the other part — what has **not** been settled, kept in the open rather than quietly asserted.

Two kinds of thing are listed:

- **Marked claims.** Anywhere you see the <Unverified>Like this one: an icon in the text, with a note
  saying what could not be confirmed.</Unverified> icon, the claim is on this list. Most are things a
  script cannot decide — how the engine behaves at runtime, or whether a bug still bites.
- **Open work**, below: whole areas that have not had the same treatment yet.

If you settle one at the keyboard, the fix is usually a one-line change on the page it came from.

## Open work

### Directions that depend on where something sits on a map

The guide now describes **RPU 2.4.x** throughout. Going from 2.3.34 to 2.4.34 changes 2042 files,
but only 44 of them carry text or script: the rest is Pixote's remade map art, and not one critter
or item proto moves, so no stat, XP, damage or price figure in the guide is affected. Of the 44,
three facts reached these pages and all three have been fixed.

**What needs doing:** the remaining risk is not numbers but *directions* — "north of the white
shed", "the locker on the left". Those were written against the old maps, and the EPA storage room
turned out to be one of them.

The scope is now measured rather than guessed. 170 of the 176 maps changed, but comparing the two
releases object by object shows what that change *is*: scenery is cut by 10–40% on every town map,
while **critters, Misc and containers stay put**. Re-tiling, not re-planning. So a direction that
points at a person or a container is almost always safe, and one that points at decorative
scenery — a shed, a barrel, a parked truck — is the kind that rots.

Checked and clear:

| Page | Result |
| --- | --- |
| The Den | every critter identical across `denbus1`, `denbus2`, `denres1`; gravesites identical |
| Broken Hills | every critter identical across `broken1`, `broken2` |
| Redding | one Gun Guard moved a hex in `reddown`; nothing the guide mentions |
| EPA | the one page that did drift — see the plant counts, now fixed |

The landmark directions on those pages have now been read against the 2.4 maps too, one object at
a time rather than one map at a time:

| Claim | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Den: the graveyard east of the church | right — and the graveyard renders to the right of the church, matching |
| Den: a barrel south of the graveyard | right — barrels at row 123 against the graveyard's 100–116 |
| Den: Tubby and Flick on the east side | **wrong, they are west** — fixed |
| Broken Hills: Chad's house at the far end of the east side | right — the briefcase container sits on the east map, though Chad himself stands downtown |
| Broken Hills: a carpet two houses east of Eric's | right — a Ladder shares its hex with a Worn Rug |
| Broken Hills: a manhole in the field | right — a Sewer Hole on the east map, not the unrelated Manhole on the west one |
| Redding: Modjeska south of the saloon | right |
| Redding: the well south of Sheriff Marion's | right |

**One thing to know before repeating any of this: Fallout's hex grid is mirrored.** A *lower*
column is further east, and a lower row is further north. Taking the intuitive reading instead
turns every correct east–west claim above into a wrong one, which is worth more than it sounds:
the first two Den claims would both have been "corrected" into errors.

Both of those have since been settled, and they went opposite ways. The Den's outhouse is **real**:
it stands north of the Hole exactly as described, assembled from wall pieces rather than being one
"Outhouse" object, which is why nothing finds it by name. Redding's "next to the well" was
**wrong** — the well is the mine entrance some thirty hexes off, and what stands in Widow Rooney's
yard is a water tank. Both are fixed on their pages.

**The directions are now done.** Of the pages still carrying one, most sit on maps that did not
change at all between the releases — Klamath's Trapper Town, the Duntons' square, Vault City's
courtyard and the whole of Navarro are byte-identical, so nothing on them can have drifted. Where
the map *did* change, the claim and the change turned out not to meet: the Abbey's basement moved
two monks north–south while its claims are "east section" and "west basement"; the Enclave moved
two unnamed guards while its claims are about a corridor, an alcove and a staircase; Klamath's
canyon moved a single critter by one hex. Abbey's west basement was checked properly anyway and
Hank really is in it.

That leaves no known-stale direction in the guide. What remains under this heading is the risk
rather than a list: a landmark that was redrawn rather than moved will not show up in any of this,
because nothing compares scenery by position.

**A name search cannot settle a scenery claim either way, and that is the method for the rest.**
Twice the object did not carry the name the guide uses: Broken Hills' "manhole" is a `Sewer Hole`,
the Den's outhouse is a shed built from walls. Once the name matched something real and
irrelevant — an actual `Manhole`, on the wrong map, which would have made a correct claim look
wrong. Find the thing the claim is really about, by script or by rendering the place and looking,
and only then compare positions. And remember the grid is mirrored: lower column is further east,
lower row further north.

The item counts move by one or two on three maps (a Locker, Tool Board and Workbench gone from
Broken Hills downtown, a Bookcase from the residential map, a Knife added in the Den), and none is
a container the guide sends you to.

**Directions that point at a person are now settled, everywhere.** Every one of the 176 maps was
read out of both releases and compared critter by critter: **145 are identical**, and of the 31
that differ, most move a single wanderer by a hex. Taking the whole game together, only **15
critters moved more than eight hexes east or west** — the axis a "west side of town" claim turns
on — and every one of them is ambient life with no name: five New Reno junkies, three
prostitutes, two Enclave guards, two scorpions, a rat, an EPA critter and a Hubologist guard. Not
one named NPC moved anywhere. Some maps shifted wholesale rather than internally — the Vault City
village slid about three rows, the desert encounter exactly 600 hexes — which moves every hex
number while leaving every direction between things intact.

**Still to do:** the landmarks that are *scenery*, which is the part the remake actually rewrote
and the part the EPA's white shed belonged to. A shed, a barrel, a parked truck or a fence can be
gone or redrawn without anything else moving, and no comparison here covers them, because the
tooling reports scenery by kind and count rather than by position. That wants eyes on a render, or
a walk through the map, page by page.

`mounts` must report `rpuVersion: v2.4.34` before any of this counts.

### Pages checked only where they mention companions

The walkthrough was corrected wherever a companion was involved, which is not the same as being
checked. Quest steps, XP figures, skill checks and directions that never mention a companion have not
been read against the scripts.

**The skill and stat checks are done.** Every `has_skill` gate in the released scripts was pulled
out and compared with the numbers on the pages that quote one — the EPA, New Reno, Vault City, NCR,
the Abbey, the Den, Gecko, Klamath, Arroyo and San Francisco, about fifty figures. They are right,
and right in a way that is easy to get wrong: a script asking `> 80` is quoted as **81%**, and
where the page says "above 80%" the script really is `> 80`. The guide has been consistent about
the difference between the threshold and the number beside it.

One figure is now marked instead: Myron's jet-cure line asks for IN 7 and Science 81%, and nothing
in `nhmyron.ssl` has that shape.

**The money figures look right too, though they do not sweep as cleanly.** All 136 literal
`dude_caps_adjust` awards were pulled out and matched against the sums the pages print. Most of
what did not match turns out to be money going the other way — the $48,000 Phoenix implant, the
$120 you hand Widow Rooney, the $60 for Fannie Mae — which an awards list cannot contain by
definition. Sampled three of those against their scripts and all three are exact: `rcrooney.ssl`
really does ask for `dude_caps < 120` and take 120, and pays the 100 the page quotes. No error
turned up, but unlike the skill gates this one is a sample rather than a proof, because the guide's
`$` figures mix rewards, prices and totals and only the rewards are mechanically checkable.

What is still unread is the rest — quest steps, item placements and the prose around them on pages
no companion ever touches.

**What needs doing:** a page-by-page pass, in the order the walkthrough runs. `check-rpu-drift.py`
already catches quests and XP figures that no script awards; the rest is reading.

### Engine claims inherited from the 1998 executable

The guide inherited a set of bugs and tricks from Per Jorner's guide, written for the original
executable. Many are fixed in FOR:CE, and the audited ones say so — see
[Known Issues](/reference/known-issues). The rest are neither confirmed nor ruled out, because they
depend on runtime behaviour that reading the source does not settle; they are in the list below,
alongside everything else still open.

**What needs doing:** playing. Each of these is a save, a reload and five minutes.

### Nothing here has been verified in play

Every fact in this guide comes from reading the game's files and the engine's source. That is a good
way to get numbers and conditions right, and a poor way to notice that something does not work the
way the code implies. Where those two can disagree, the claim is marked.

### [San Francisco](/walkthrough/san-francisco)

- [ ] [Always use **end turn**, never end combat.](/walkthrough/san-francisco#martial-arts-training) *(Martial arts training)*<br />
  **Why it is unconfirmed:** The window is narrow and specific. `fcdragon.ssl` notices you have won inside `combat_p_proc`, which only runs while combat is running: when the opponent drops below a quarter of their hit points it arms a timer, and that timer is the only thing that ever gives your inventory back, heals you and moves you out of the ring — during a test `ReturnInven` is reached only from `endTestPass` and `endTestFail`, and nothing but those timers calls either. Killing outright is covered rather than missed: a dead, prone or knocked-out opponent is scored as 0 hit points out of 4, so it counts as below the quarter and arms the timer exactly like a blow that leaves them standing. What is at risk is not the kill but the Dragon's next turn, since his is the `combat_p_proc` doing the looking. If combat is still going when the timer fires it calls `terminate_combat` and re-arms itself, so ordinarily it cannot lose you — but end combat by hand before the opponent's health is ever checked and no timer is armed at all, and nothing is left to restore you. That is the shape of the reported bug, and it is consistent with it. Still not reproduced, though the ring fight itself has now been played: the Dragon will only teach you at 250 karma or better, and a test is a separate dialogue branch from the lessons. Through it the ordinary path is sturdy — the timer arms, the next opponent is fetched, the fight resumes on the ring tile. The end-combat attempt is simply refused throughout, since Fallout 2 binds it to the same key that declines to end a fight anyone is still engaged in, and in the ring the Dragon's men and the Chinatown crowd are all in combat. Whether a quieter ring lets the key through, and with it the window above, is the part still open.
